VCH EchoCalc — Support & Help

Help, answers and contact details for the VCH EchoCalc iOS app — the echocardiography calculator for veterinary professionals. Operated by David Evans, T/A VetCardioHub, established in Wales, UK.

Contact us

If you need help, have a question that isn't answered below, want to report a problem, or think a reference interval or model is wrong, we'd be glad to hear from you. We aim to reply within a few working days.

Email: info@vetcardiohub.com

The quickest route is Studies ▸ About & clinical disclaimer ▸ Report an issue in the app, which opens an email pre-filled with your app version and the dates of the reference set and glossary you are running. Those three figures are the first thing we need to answer "this number looks wrong".

Please do not email patient-identifiable information. A description of the measurements and the model selected is enough to reproduce almost any calculation question.

Important: we can help with how the app works and with the provenance of the models it uses, but we cannot give clinical advice on an individual patient by email, and nothing we say by email forms a second opinion. For case-specific advice, please refer to or consult a board-certified cardiologist in the normal way.

Getting started & the basics

What is VCH EchoCalc?

VCH EchoCalc is the native iOS version of the VetCardioHub echocardiography calculator. You enter the measurements from a study and it derives the standard indices, compares them against published reference intervals and scoring systems, and assembles a report you can copy, share or export as a PDF. It is written for qualified veterinary professionals and assumes competence in echocardiography.

It classifies measurements against published intervals. It does not diagnose, and it cannot see the patient, the images, or the quality of the study behind the numbers.

How is the app laid out?

Five tabs along the bottom of the screen:

  • Measure — patient details, weight, model selection and every measurement field, grouped into collapsible sections.
  • Results — the derived indices, each with its reference interval and a normal / borderline / abnormal marker.
  • Assess — the scoring engines: diastolic function, pulmonary hypertension, MINE, and the species-specific assessments.
  • Report — the full text report and the PH audit, with share, copy and PDF export.
  • Studies — saved studies, practice branding, and About with the clinical disclaimer and legal documents.
Does it cost anything, and does it need an account or the internet?

The app is free. There is no account to create, no sign-in and no subscription. It works fully offline — every calculation, reference interval and score is computed on your device from data built into the app.

The only thing it ever fetches is an updated glossary from vetcardiohub.com. That request sends us nothing about you, your practice or any patient, and if it fails the app carries on with its built-in copy.

What is the ⓘ button next to a field?

It opens the glossary entry for that measurement or index: what it is, the optimal view or source, the technique or formula, and the citation where one is recorded. The same button appears beside result rows and beside items in the pulmonary hypertension audit.

Entering measurements

What units does it expect?

Linear dimensions in millimetres, velocities in m/s, times in ms, volumes in ml, areas in cm², and VTIs in cm. The one exception is E′ (tissue Doppler), which is entered in cm/s — it is the only cm/s field, and the unit is shown beside the box.

Each field shows its unit on screen, and implausible entries are flagged inline — for example a left ventricular dimension under 8 mm prompts "Is this in cm? Convert to mm."

Why does weight matter so much?

Almost everything scales off it. Body surface area, LVIDdn, LADn, the right-heart intervals and both volumetric tables are all functions of body weight, so a weight error propagates into every normalised value on the report. Because the exponents are fractional, a 10% weight error shifts LVIDdn by roughly 3% — small, but enough to move a borderline patient across the 1.7 EPIC cut-off.

Weigh at the time of the study where you can, and treat estimated or historical weights with caution.

Can I paste a report from the ultrasound machine?

Yes. Tap the paste icon at the top of the Measure tab, paste the text of a machine-generated report, and the app will read what it recognises and show you exactly which fields it proposes to fill, with the values, before anything is applied. Fields you have already typed are never overwritten.

Check every parsed value against the source report before you rely on it. This is pattern-matching on text, not a validated import: it can mis-read a label, take a ratio for a measurement, or assign a value to the wrong field, particularly with vendor formats it has not seen. The preview exists precisely so you can catch that.

How do I clear everything for the next patient?

At the bottom of the Measure tab, Clear all measurements. Measurements, patient details and the subjective findings are cleared; your model selections are deliberately kept, because they are a preference rather than patient data.

Models & reference intervals

Which allometric model should I choose?

The model is yours to choose, and it changes the answer — the same measurements scored against a different model can cross a clinical threshold in either direction. The app defaults to the standard canine (Cornell) model and offers non-sighthound, sighthound, toy-breed, puppy, adult cat and kitten alternatives. Each one shows its reference and PMID on screen, under Assess ▸ Models and provenance.

Pick the model whose published cohort your patient actually resembles, and be cautious applying any interval to a patient of different signalment, body condition or training status from that cohort.

Why did the model change when I picked a breed?

Selecting certain breeds switches the allometric model automatically and tells you it has done so — sighthounds to the Stepien sighthound model, cats to Karsten, toy breeds to Isayama, and the kitten cohort to Visser. This mirrors the web calculator. The selection remains yours: change it back if you disagree.

How do I use the breed reference tables?

Assess ▸ Breed reference shows the published breed-specific values alongside your patient's, one table per source study, with the citation for each. Where a study published a median and standard deviation rather than an interval, the app derives the interval as median ± 1.96 SD and says so on screen — that is a methodological disclosure, not decoration.

Breeds known to sit outside general reference intervals are flagged as deviant, which is the whole reason to consult these tables for those patients.

Why is a value showing no reference interval?

Because none is published for that parameter in the model you have selected. The app shows "—" or omits the interval rather than borrowing one from elsewhere. In particular it will never apply a canine model to a cat or vice versa as a fallback: the volumetric (Simpson's) reference tables are canine only, and return "unavailable" for cats by design.

Results & the scoring engines

Why is a row missing from the Results tab?

Because the measurement it needs has not been entered. A row that cannot be calculated is omitted entirely rather than shown as zero — an unmeasured field and a measured zero are different things, and the app never lets them look the same.

What do the diastolic function grades mean?

The classifier is canine only. It matches up to eight parameters against published bands and resolves them into Normal, Grade I (impaired relaxation), Grade II (pseudonormal) or Grade III (restrictive). It needs at least two parameters before it will grade at all, and it shows every contributing parameter with its band so you can see what drove the result.

It also reports a confidence label. Where parameters disagree, it says so rather than presenting a clean answer — and it deliberately does not treat a "normal" E:A, IVRT or MDT inside a Grade II or III result as a contradiction, because those pseudo-normalise by definition.

How is the pulmonary hypertension probability worked out?

It follows the ACVIM (2020) consensus approach: the tricuspid regurgitation velocity where you have one, plus the count of anatomic sites (ventricles, pulmonary artery, right atrium) showing supportive changes. The app shows the full grouped evidence, marks each item normal or abnormal against its threshold, and states how many of the three sites are positive.

Where no TR jet is measurable, the Chang (2026) predictive score can raise the probability to intermediate. That override is shown explicitly in the result, so you can always see why a probability was reached.

Which PA:Ao ratio does the Chang score use?

The short-axis ratio — main pulmonary artery to aorta, as the paper describes. If you have entered the short-axis pair, that is what the score uses. The long-axis RVOT:Ao ratio is used only as a fallback, so a study that recorded only the long-axis pair still contributes rather than scoring zero.

What does the MINE score tell me — and what does it not?

MINE is a prognostic score for dogs with myxomatous mitral valve disease, and the app offers both the 2021 four-parameter and 2025 three-parameter versions. It reports a stage and the median survival time published for that stage.

That figure is a population statistic, not a prediction for the animal in front of you. It carries wide individual variation and says nothing about response to treatment. It should not be relayed to an owner as an estimate of their dog's remaining lifespan. The app scores all-or-nothing: if any required variable is missing it reports "awaiting inputs" rather than a partial score.

Why do some panels say "dogs only"?

Because the underlying models are canine. The diastolic classifier, the ACVIM pulmonary hypertension criteria, the Chang score and MINE are all canine; selecting a feline model switches the Assess tab to the feline assessments instead — HCM phenotype by wall thickness, atrial thromboembolism risk by left atrial diameter, and outflow tract obstruction by aortic velocity. Nothing is applied across species as a substitute.

Reports, PDFs & practice branding

How do I get the report out of the app?

The Report tab builds it as you go. The share button offers Share as text (into an email, a note or your practice management system), Share as PDF, and Copy text. The text is also selectable on screen if you want to lift a single section.

Where the pulmonary hypertension engine has something to report, a second segment gives the full PH audit as a separate document.

Can I put my practice letterhead on the PDF?

Yes — Studies ▸ About ▸ Practice branding. You can set a practice name and address line, add a logo (placed top-right of the first page and scaled to fit), and add a signature image with a name and qualifications. These are stored on your device and applied to every PDF you export.

A signature image is applied by whoever is holding the device. It records who the report is from; it does not verify who prepared or approved it. On a shared practice iPad, turn the signature block off.

Is the report a clinical document?

It is a draft assembled from what you entered. When you share, export or file it, you do so as its author — please review it for accuracy and completeness before it enters a clinical record or goes to a colleague. Every page carries the date of the reference set that produced it, so an older report remains interpretable after the models are updated.

Saved studies & rechecks

Does the app save studies automatically?

No. Saving is deliberately an explicit act — Studies ▸ Save current study — so that nothing you type is written to a stored record behind your back. Everything stays on your device.

What does "start a recheck" do?

It sets up a fresh study for the same patient: the name, owner, breed and your model selections carry over, and every measurement starts empty — including weight. Weight is cleared deliberately, because it scales every allometric interval and a recheck is usually the visit at which it has changed.

Why does a saved report differ from the same study reloaded?

Because a saved report is kept exactly as it read when you saved it, together with the date of the reference set that produced it. Loading the study back into the calculator recalculates it against the current reference set. If those two differ, the models have been updated in between — which is information worth having, not an error.

Updates, privacy & your data

What updates by itself, and what needs an app update?

The glossary refreshes over the air: the app checks vetcardiohub.com occasionally, adopts a newer copy on the next launch, and falls back to its built-in version if it cannot connect.

The reference set — every allometric model, breed table, volumetric table and scoring rule — is built into the app and changes only with an app update. The same is true of the Terms and Privacy Policy. You can see the version of each under Studies ▸ About.

Where is patient data stored, and who can see it?

On your device, and only on your device. Measurements, patient and owner names, saved studies, generated reports and any logo or signature image stay in the app's own storage. Nothing is transmitted to us, there is no account and no cloud sync, and we cannot see or recover any of it.

Because those records may identify your clients, you and your practice are the data controller for them. Device passcode and encryption matter, and anything that belongs in the clinical record should be exported into it rather than left in the app. Full details are in our Privacy Policy.

What happens if I delete the app or change device?

Saved studies are deleted permanently and irrecoverably, except to the extent they are captured in your device's own encrypted backups. There is no cloud copy for us to restore. Export anything you need to keep.

Troubleshooting

A calculated value looks wrong.

Check three things in order: the units of the entered value (E′ is cm/s; everything else linear is mm), the body weight, and which allometric model is selected. Between them these explain almost every surprising number. If it still looks wrong, please email us with the measurements, the model and the version details from About — the calculator's output is checked against the web version automatically, so a genuine discrepancy is something we want to know about.

A score says "awaiting inputs" or "awaiting metrics".

That engine has not been given enough to work with. The diastolic classifier needs at least two of its eight parameters; MINE needs all of its variables before it will report a stage; the Chang score needs all six of its structural measurements. Each panel lists what it is using, so you can see what is missing.

The glossary or a ⓘ button seems out of date or missing.

The glossary refreshes in the background and adopts a newer copy on the next launch, so closing and reopening the app usually resolves it. If a field has no ⓘ button at all, no glossary entry exists for it yet — please tell us which one and we will write it.

The PDF will not export, or the letterhead is missing.

The report has to contain something first — enter at least a weight or a patient name. If the letterhead is missing, check that a practice name or logo is set under Studies ▸ About ▸ Practice branding; if the signature block is missing, check that the signature switch is on. The text report is always available as a fallback.
