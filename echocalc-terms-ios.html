5. Reference intervals, allometric models and scoring systems

The Application applies published models — allometric reference intervals, volumetric lookup tables, breed-specific tables, diastolic classification rules, pulmonary hypertension probability criteria and predictive scores — as described in the literature it cites on screen. You should be aware that:

  • Model selection is yours, and it changes the answer. The same measurements scored against a different allometric model, or against a breed-specific table rather than a general one, can cross a clinical threshold in either direction. Selecting a breed may automatically change the model; the app tells you when it does, and the selection remains yours to review.

  • Reference intervals describe populations, not individuals. A value inside an interval does not exclude disease, and a value outside one does not establish it.

  • The published source may not fit your patient. Reference intervals derive from specific cohorts, imaging protocols and measurement conventions. Applying them to a patient of different signalment, body condition, training status or haemodynamic state may not be valid.

  • Citations are shown so you can check them. Each model, table and score displays its reference and, where recorded, its PMID. Where no identifier is recorded, the app says so rather than implying one exists.

  • Values are only as good as the measurements. Off-axis planes, wrong-frame calipers, incorrect units and Doppler misalignment all produce numbers the app will accept and score without complaint.

We make every reasonable effort to transcribe published constants accurately and to keep them current, but we do not warrant that any reference interval, threshold or model implementation is free from error, is the most recent published version, or is appropriate for any particular patient. Verify anything that will change your management against the primary literature.

6. Prognostic and probability estimates

Certain features present population-level statistics, including estimated median survival times and probability categories for pulmonary hypertension. These are group estimates drawn from published cohorts. They are not a prediction of what will happen to the animal in front of you, they carry wide individual variation, and they say nothing about how any particular patient will respond to treatment. They must never be communicated to a client as a prediction of that animal’s lifespan. Any such figure should be discussed in the context of the whole clinical picture, and, where appropriate, with a board-certified cardiologist.
